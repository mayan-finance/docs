Getting money into an app and getting it back out are the two moments that decide whether someone uses your product.

## How it works

Funding used to mean working backwards from whatever the app accepted: move to the right chain, swap into the right asset, then deposit. Leaving meant the same in reverse, with waiting added.

On Mayan the user starts from any supported token on any supported chain, and on the way out picks the chain and the token they want. Funds go straight to their wallet.

A trader holding SOL on Solana deposits into a venue, trades, and leaves holding ETH on Ethereum, USDC on Base, or anything else.

## Deposit addresses

When the same user funds again and again, Mayan can issue them an address that does not expire.

They send to it like any other transfer, with no wallet connection, no signing and no approvals, and your product receives the asset you configured. See [Deposit Addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## Examples

- A DEX lets users fund from the asset they already hold rather than the one it settles in.
- An exchange with internal balances drops the conversion step users do by hand.
- An onramp or checkout takes whatever the user brings and settles in the asset the business runs on.
- A vault accepts deposits in assets it does not otherwise support.
- A product that pays users out lets them receive in the asset and on the chain they want.

## Case studies

- **Hyperliquid.** Deposits and withdrawals in one move, from any chain and any asset. See [Hyperliquid](/integration/hyperliquid).
