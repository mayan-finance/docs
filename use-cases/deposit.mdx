Getting money into an app and getting it back out are the two moments that decide whether someone uses your product.

On Mayan both are a single move. Users arrive with whatever they already hold, and they leave holding whatever they actually want.

## One move in

Funding an account used to mean working backwards from whatever the app accepted:

- Move funds to the chain the app supports
- Swap into the asset it accepts
- Then deposit
- Across separate interfaces, with waiting in between

On Mayan the user starts from any supported token on any supported chain, and the funds land ready to use in seconds.

## One move out

Leaving was the same problem with waiting added:

- Convert into the one asset the venue holds
- Withdraw
- Wait
- Swap again somewhere else

On Mayan the user picks the chain and the token they want, and the funds move straight to their wallet.

## The round trip

A trader starts with SOL on Solana, deposits into a venue, trades, and leaves holding ETH on Ethereum, USDC on Base, or anything else they want.

<Info>
  **What this means:** any token, any chain in. Any token, any chain out.
</Info>

## It's just a send

When the same user funds again and again, Mayan can give them an address that does not expire.

They send to it like any other transfer, with no wallet connection, no signing, and no approvals, and your product receives the asset you configured. See [Deposit Addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## How teams use it

- **DEXs and trading venues** let users fund from the asset they already hold rather than the one the venue accepts.
- **Exchanges and apps with internal balances** drop the conversion step users currently do by hand.
- **Onramps and checkout flows** take whatever the user brings and settle in the asset the business runs on.
- **Vaults and protocols** accept deposits in assets they do not otherwise support.
- **Any product that pays users out** lets them receive in the asset and on the chain they want.

See [Solutions](/solutions/applications) for how each of those teams sets it up.
