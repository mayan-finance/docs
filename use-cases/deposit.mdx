A deposit is a swap whose destination address is an account inside your app. A withdrawal is the same call in reverse: funds leave your app, and the user picks the chain and token they receive.

The user does not need to hold the asset your app settles in, and does not need to be on your chain before they start.

## How it works

Pass your app's deposit address as `destinationAddress` and the asset your app accepts as `toToken`. Whatever the user holds is converted on the way in, and arrives as one transfer.

```typescript
const quotes = await fetchQuote({
  amountIn64: "5000000000",
  fromChain: "solana",
  fromToken: SOL,            // whatever the user holds
  toChain: "arbitrum",
  toToken: USDC_ARBITRUM,    // the asset your app settles in
  slippageBps: "auto",
});
```

Withdrawals invert it: your app is the source, and the user supplies the destination chain and token.

## Deposit addresses

For users who fund repeatedly, Mayan can issue a permanent address instead of a per-transfer quote. The user sends to it like any ordinary transfer, with no wallet connection, no signature and no approval, and your app receives the asset you configured.

Set `mpsDeposit: true` alongside your `destinationAddress` on the quote request, and the response returns an `mpsDepositAddress`. See [deposit addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## Examples

- A DEX lets users fund from the asset they already hold rather than the one it settles in.
- An exchange with internal balances removes the conversion step users otherwise do by hand.
- An onramp or checkout accepts whatever the user brings and settles in one asset.
- A vault accepts deposits in assets it does not otherwise support.
- A product that pays users out lets them receive in the asset and on the chain they want.
