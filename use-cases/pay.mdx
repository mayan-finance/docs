A payment on Mayan is a swap that settles in the asset the receiver runs on. The payer sends whatever they hold, on whatever chain they hold it, and the payment lands in seconds.

Stablecoins are Mayan's most moved asset, with over **\$8B** moved cross-chain. Fast MCTP routes those transfers over Circle's CCTP for ultra-low slippage.

## How it works

A buyer pays 0.16 ETH on Ethereum, and 250 USDC arrives in the merchant's account. The buyer signs one transaction and never interacts with a swap interface.

The destination chain, token and address are set by whoever is receiving, and none of them have to match what the payer holds.

## Guaranteed price

An ordinary swap quotes an expected output and a minimum, then settles between them. A payment usually needs one exact number.

On USDC, USDT and ETH routes Mayan can settle exact-out instead, so the recipient receives exactly `expectedAmountOut` and the amount you display is the amount that lands. See [guaranteed price](/features/guaranteed-price).

## Repeat payers

Where the same payer sends again and again, they can pay to a fixed address instead of signing each time. See [deposit addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## Examples

- A fintech accepts many assets and settles in one.
- A payroll or remittance app pays recipients in the asset and on the chain they choose.
- A merchant checkout displays a price and receives that exact amount.
- A wallet adding payments lets users send without picking a chain first.

## Case studies

- **PrimeVault** rebalances stablecoin reserves across ecosystems and pays a vendor on one chain from a treasury on another, without assets leaving the vault.
