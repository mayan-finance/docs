A payment is a swap where the recipient is someone other than the sender, and where the output amount usually has to be exact.

Stablecoins are Mayan's most moved asset, with over **\$8B** moved cross-chain. Fast MCTP routes those transfers over Circle's CCTP for ultra-low slippage.

## How it works

`destinationAddress` is the recipient rather than the payer. The payer's token and chain are the inputs, the recipient's token and chain are the outputs, and neither side has to match the other.

A buyer pays 0.16 ETH on Ethereum; 250 USDC arrives in the merchant's account. The buyer signs one transaction and never interacts with a swap interface.

## Fixing the output amount

An ordinary swap quotes an expected output and a minimum, and settles somewhere between them. A payment usually needs an exact number.

On USDC, USDT and ETH routes, Mayan supports exact-out settlement. Request `swiftAuctionMode: 3` and the recipient receives exactly `expectedAmountOut`; drivers bid on how much input they need rather than how much output they deliver. See [guaranteed price](/features/guaranteed-price).

## Repeat payers

Where the same payer sends again and again, they can pay to a fixed address instead of signing each time. See [deposit addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## Examples

- A fintech accepts many assets and settles in one.
- A payroll or remittance app pays recipients in the asset and on the chain they want.
- A merchant checkout displays a price and receives that exact amount.
- A wallet adding payments lets users send without picking a chain first.

## Case studies

- **PrimeVault** rebalances stablecoin reserves across ecosystems and pays a vendor on one chain from a treasury on another, without assets leaving the vault.
