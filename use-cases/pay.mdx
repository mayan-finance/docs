An onchain payment has two requirements a trade does not. The amount has to be exact, because a business cannot bill one number and receive another. And it has to arrive now, because nobody waits at a checkout.

Mayan covers both. Stablecoins are its most moved asset, with over **\$8B** moved cross-chain, and Fast MCTP routes those transfers over Circle's CCTP for ultra-low slippage.

## How it works

The payer sends from the asset and chain they already hold. The payment settles in the stablecoin the business runs on, on the chain it runs on. A buyer pays 0.16 ETH on Ethereum, and 250 USDC arrives in the merchant's account.

The buyer signs one transaction and never interacts with a swap interface.

## Guaranteed price

An ordinary swap quotes an expected output and a minimum, then settles somewhere between them. That range is fine for a trade and unusable for a payment.

On USDC, USDT and ETH routes Mayan settles exact-out instead: the recipient receives exactly `expectedAmountOut`, so the amount you display is the amount that lands. See [guaranteed price](/features/guaranteed-price).

## Repeat payers

Where the same payer sends again and again, they can pay to a fixed address instead of signing each time. See [deposit addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).

## Examples

- A fintech accepts many assets and settles in one.
- A payroll or remittance app pays recipients in the asset and on the chain they choose.
- A merchant checkout displays a price and receives that exact amount.
- A wallet adding payments lets users send without picking a chain first.

## Case studies

- **PrimeVault** rebalances stablecoin reserves across ecosystems and pays a vendor on one chain from a treasury on another, without assets leaving the vault. [Read more](https://mayan.finance/blog/mayan-unlocks-native-cross-chain-transfers-for-institutions-on-primevault).
