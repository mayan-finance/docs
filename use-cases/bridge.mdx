The user says where their assets are and where they need to be, and it arrives in seconds.

## Moving between chains stops feeling like a step

It used to be its own task: move to the chain the destination supported, wait, land holding a wrapped asset, then find gas before you could use it.

On Mayan the user lands holding the asset they actually asked for. [Gas on destination](/application/gas-on-destination) puts native gas in their wallet on arrival, and [gasless execution](/features/gasless-execution) can take the gas step off the front on supported routes. Transfers route up to \$10M, so size does not change the experience either.

Most people never notice any of it. A large share of the moves Mayan settles happen without the user knowing Mayan was the route.

## Secured by a decentralized messaging network

Every cross-chain move depends on one chain being told, reliably, what happened on another.

Wormhole's Guardian network attests each message, requiring 13 of 19 independent organizations to sign before it is valid. The driver delivers on the destination chain first and only unlocks the source funds once that delivery is verified, so the risk sits with them rather than the user.

<Info>
  **What this means:** the move runs through public, audited contracts rather than infrastructure you cannot inspect. See [Security & Audits](/resources/audits).
</Info>

## How teams use it

- Wallets let users move holdings between chains without leaving the app.
- Chains become reachable from every major ecosystem on day one.
- Apps and aggregators meet users wherever they already hold assets.
