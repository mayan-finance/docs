The user says where their assets are and where they need to be, and it arrives in seconds.

## How it works

A cross-chain move used to be its own task: bridge to the chain the destination supported, wait, land holding a wrapped asset, then find gas before you could use it.

On Mayan the user lands holding the asset they asked for. [Gas on destination](/application/gas-on-destination) puts native gas in their wallet on arrival, and [gasless execution](/features/gasless-execution) removes the gas step at the start on supported routes. Transfers route up to \$10M, so size does not change the experience either.

## Connected by Wormhole

Every cross-chain move depends on one chain being told, reliably, what happened on another.

[Wormhole's](https://wormhole.com) Guardian network attests each message, requiring 13 of 19 independent organizations to sign before it is valid. The driver delivers on the destination chain first and only unlocks the source funds once that delivery is verified, so the risk sits with the driver rather than the user.

The contracts are public and audited. See [Security & Audits](/resources/audits).

## Examples

- A wallet lets users move holdings between chains without leaving the app.
- A new chain becomes reachable from every major ecosystem on day one.
- An app meets users wherever they already hold their assets.
