The person paying and the business getting paid rarely hold the same thing.

Mayan converts between them on the way, so the buyer pays with whatever is in their wallet and you receive the asset your books run on.

## The buyer pays in anything, you settle in one thing

A customer pays 0.16 ETH on Ethereum, and you receive 250 USDC. They never see a swap, and you never handle a token you did not ask for.

## A price that holds

Guaranteed Price locks the output before the payment is confirmed on USDC, USDT and ETH routes, so the amount you display is the amount that lands. That is what a checkout needs. See [Guaranteed Price](/features/guaranteed-price).

## See it in action

- **MoonPay** converts onramp deposits into any token instantly, powered by Mayan.
- **PrimeVault** manages stablecoin treasury operations across any chain, powered by Mayan.

- [Pay](/use-cases/pay), for stablecoin settlement where the amount matters
