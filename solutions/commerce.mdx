Mayan sits underneath payment and commerce flows, converting what the buyer holds into what the business settles in. Neither side handles a swap.

The person paying and the business getting paid rarely hold the same thing.

## Accept anything, settle in one asset

A customer pays 0.16 ETH on Ethereum, and you receive 250 USDC. They never see a swap, and you never handle a token you did not ask for.

## A price that holds

On USDC, USDT and ETH routes, [Guaranteed Price](/features/guaranteed-price) locks the output before the payment is confirmed, so the amount you display is the amount that lands. That is what a checkout needs.

## Repeat payers do not have to sign

For a customer who pays again and again, Mayan can issue a fixed address. They send to it like any other transfer, and you receive the asset you configured. See [Deposit Addresses](/features/deposit-addresses).
